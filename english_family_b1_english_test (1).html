An Unexpected Journey to Eco-Living

When Sarah moved from her noisy downtown apartment to a small village in Oregon, she expected a peaceful life. What she didn't expect was how radically her lifestyle would change. The village of Oakwood had implemented a community zero-waste policy three years prior, requiring all residents to sort, compost, and recycle virtually everything they consumed.

At first, Sarah found the rules overwhelming. Buying groceries meant bringing her own cloth bags and glass jars for rice, pasta, and oil. However, after two months, these daily routines became second nature. She discovered that cooking fresh, locally grown food from the weekly farmer's market saved her money and dramatically improved her health.

Last month, Sarah organized a community workshop to teach newcomers how to repair broken household items instead of discarding them. Over forty neighbors attended. Sarah realized that living sustainably was not just about reducing trash—it was about building strong connections with the people around her.

1. Why did Sarah initially move to Oakwood?

2. How did Sarah feel about Oakwood's rules at the beginning?

3. What was one personal benefit Sarah experienced after adapting?

4. What was the purpose of the workshop Sarah organized?

5. What main conclusion did Sarah reach about sustainable living?
